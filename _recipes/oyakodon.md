## Ingredients

**Main**
- ½ yellow onion (4 oz / 113 g), peeled
- 10 oz (280 g) boneless, skinless chicken thighs (1–2 thighs)
- 1 tbsp sake (or Chinese rice wine / dry sherry, or omit)
- 3–4 large eggs, at room temperature

**Seasonings**
- ½ cup dashi
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 2 tsp sugar

**To serve**
- 2 servings cooked Japanese short-grain rice (about 250 g per serving)
- 4 sprigs mitsuba (Japanese parsley), or spring onions
- Shichimi togarashi (optional)
- Sansho pepper (optional)

## Method

**Prepare**

1. Stir together the dashi, soy sauce, mirin and sugar until the sugar dissolves.
2. Slice the onion lengthwise into ¼-inch (6 mm) pieces. Chop the mitsuba into ½-inch (1.3 cm)
   pieces.
3. Trim excess fat from the chicken and cut it into bite-size pieces (¾–1 inch / 2–2.5 cm),
   slicing at a diagonal angle (sogigiri). Drizzle with the sake and leave for 5 minutes.
4. Crack the eggs into a bowl. With chopsticks, lift and cut through the whites 5–6 times —
   don't beat them. Gently break the yolks so they stay marbled with the whites.

**Cook (one 11-inch frying pan, 2 servings)**

5. With the heat off, spread the onion in a single layer in the pan and pour over just enough of
   the seasoning to cover it (use a smaller pan if it doesn't).
6. Bring to a simmer over medium heat, then spread the chicken evenly over the onion.
7. Once it's simmering again, turn down to medium-low and cook uncovered for about 5 minutes,
   flipping the chicken halfway, until it's cooked through and the onion is tender.
8. Turn up to medium. When the sauce is gently simmering, drizzle two-thirds of the egg (mostly
   whites) in a circle over the chicken, avoiding the hot edges of the pan. Nudge any spreading
   egg back toward the centre; cover briefly if the whites need help setting.
9. When the first egg starts to set, pour over the remaining third (mostly yolks) and scatter
   over the mitsuba. Cook on medium-low until the eggs are done to your liking.
10. Divide the rice between two bowls, spoon the chicken and egg on top and drizzle with sauce.
    Eat straight away, with shichimi togarashi or sansho if you like.

## Notes

- **One bowl at a time:** in a small pan or oyakodon pan, halve everything and cook each serving
  separately (about 4 minutes for the chicken), then slide it onto the rice.
- Don't cook more than 2 servings in an 11-inch pan — overcrowding stops the sauce reducing and
  makes it watery. For 4, use two pans or cook in batches.
- Cooking uncovered lets excess moisture evaporate, concentrating the sauce.
- Whites take longer to set than yolks, which is why most of the whites go in first.
- Only add the eggs once the sauce is gently simmering — too hot and they overcook before setting
  evenly.
- Use 1½–2 eggs per serving; the egg binds everything so it transfers neatly onto the rice.
- For runny eggs, use pasteurised eggs.
- Dashi tip: make about 4 cups and use the rest for miso soup.
- Leftovers keep 2–3 days in the fridge (rice stored separately). The chicken, onion and sauce
  (without egg) freeze for up to a month — add fresh egg when reheating.
