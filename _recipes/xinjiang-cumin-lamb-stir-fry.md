## Ingredients

**Lamb and marinade**
- 500 g (1 lb) boneless lamb leg or rump, sliced ½ cm thick
- 1 tbsp light or all-purpose soy sauce (not dark or sweet)
- 1 tbsp Shaoxing (Chinese cooking) wine
- ½ tsp cooking/kosher salt
- 1¼ tsp baking soda, sifted if lumpy
- 2 tbsp cornflour

**Spice mix**
- 2 tbsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp white sugar
- ½ tsp ground Sichuan pepper

**Stir fry**
- 4 tbsp vegetable oil (or canola or peanut oil)
- ½ cup whole dried Chinese chillies (25–30)
- 1 onion, halved and sliced 8 mm thick
- 2 tbsp finely minced ginger (about a 5 cm piece)
- 5 cloves garlic, finely sliced
- ½ cup coriander, roughly chopped
- 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds
- Plain or fried rice, to serve

## Method

1. **Marinate:** mix the lamb with the soy sauce, cooking wine, salt, baking soda and cornflour.
   Leave for 30 minutes at room temperature.
2. **Spice mix:** combine the cumin, sugar and Sichuan pepper in a small bowl.
3. **Cook the lamb:** heat 3 tbsp of the oil in a large (30 cm) non-stick pan over medium-high.
   Add half the lamb in a single layer and leave for 30 seconds, then toss for about a minute
   until lightly golden. Move to a plate. Repeat with the rest of the lamb (no extra oil).
4. **Aromatics:** heat the remaining 1 tbsp oil. Add the dried chillies, ginger and garlic and
   stir for 10 seconds. Add the onion and cook about 2 minutes, until it just starts to soften.
5. **Combine:** return the lamb to the pan, sprinkle over the spice mix and toss to coat evenly.
6. **Finish:** toss through the coriander and sesame seeds. Serve immediately with rice.

## Notes

- **Lamb:** leg or rump is best for flavour, tenderness and lower fat. Slice thinly but not paper
  thin — it should keep some bite. Backstrap is too pricey for this; shoulder or shank are too
  tough.
- **Cooking wine:** Shaoxing is best; mirin, cooking sake or dry sherry also work. Non-alcoholic:
  2 tbsp chicken stock.
- **Baking soda** tenderises the meat (Chinese velveting). The amount is small enough that you
  don't need to rinse it off.
- **Sichuan pepper** gives a cool, lemony tingle rather than heat. Pre-ground is fine. To grind
  your own, dry-toast 1½ tsp peppercorns, cool, grind, sift and measure ½ tsp. ¼ tsp white pepper
  is an alternative, though the flavour changes.
- **Dried chillies** are for aroma, not heat — don't eat them. Any Asian or Indian dried red
  chillies work.
- Leftovers keep 3–4 days in the fridge. Not suitable for freezing.
