## Ingredients

**Main**
- 2 mackerel fillets (~13 oz / 374 g total, 2 fillets from 1 mackerel)

**Seasoning #1**
- 4 tbsp water
- 4 tbsp sake
- 2 tbsp mirin
- 2 tbsp sugar
- 2 tbsp miso (red or darker miso recommended)
- 1 knob ginger, half sliced and half julienned

**Seasoning #2**
- 2 tbsp miso (red or darker miso recommended)
- 1 tsp soy sauce

## Method

**Prepare the ingredients**

1. Scrape the ginger skin with a knife or spoon and cut into thin slices. Reserve half for
   cooking; julienne the other half for garnish.
2. Cut each mackerel fillet in half at a slanted angle. Make shallow cross incisions on the skin
   side of the thickest parts.

**Blanch the fish**

3. Bring a medium pot of water to a boil. Gently add the fillets one at a time for a quick blanch
   to remove sliminess, odor, and impurities. Scoop out with a fine-mesh strainer or slotted spoon.
4. Shock the fish immediately in an ice bath. Repeat the blanch and ice bath for the remaining
   fillets.
5. Clean the fish in the ice water, removing any blood and impurities — the ice water also firms
   the flesh. Transfer to a plate and set aside.

**Cook the fish**

6. In a medium/large saucepan, add Seasoning #1 (water, sake, mirin, sugar, 2 tbsp miso, and the
   sliced ginger).
7. Turn heat to medium and bring the sauce to a boil, mixing the ingredients as it heats.
8. Once boiling, place the mackerel in a single layer, skin side up.
9. Reduce heat to medium-low and place an otoshibuta (drop lid) on top. Gently simmer 13–15
   minutes — the drop lid keeps the fish down and helps the sauce circulate.
10. Remove the drop lid. Using a fine-mesh skimmer or slotted spoon, gently transfer one fillet
    to a plate.
11. Add the remaining 2 tbsp miso (Seasoning #2) to the sauce and let it dissolve completely.
12. Return the fillet to the sauce and drizzle in the 1 tsp soy sauce.
13. Hold the pot and tilt it gently to distribute the sauce over the fish rather than stirring —
    spoon sauce over the fish a few times.
14. Turn off the heat, cover, and let the fish cool 30 minutes so it absorbs the flavors without
    overcooking.

**Reheat and serve**

15. Uncover and turn heat to medium. Reheat the fish, staying nearby to prevent the thickened
    sauce from burning, spooning sauce over the fish several times.
16. Transfer the fish to a serving plate and drizzle with the sauce.
17. Garnish with the julienned ginger (and kinome leaf, if using).

## Notes

- Blanching ("shimofuri") removes impurities and fishy odor while firming the flesh.
- Sake removes odor and evaporates off, making the fish tender and juicy.
- Adding the miso in two steps preserves its flavor, since miso loses potency with prolonged
  cooking.
- Tilt the pot gently to distribute the sauce rather than stirring — there isn't much sauce to
  work with.
- Like most Japanese simmered dishes, cooling and reheating improves flavor absorption.
- Leftovers keep in an airtight container in the fridge up to 2 days.
