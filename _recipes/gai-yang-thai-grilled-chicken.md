## Ingredients

**Chicken**
- 2 lb (900 g) boneless, skin-on chicken thighs

**Marinade**
- 5 garlic cloves
- 1 small shallot
- ½ cup coriander, including stems
- 2 tbsp palm sugar
- 3 tbsp soy sauce
- 2 tbsp sweet soy sauce
- 2 tbsp fish sauce
- ½ tsp white pepper
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tsp coriander seeds
- 1 tbsp oil

**Nam jim jaew (dipping sauce)**
- 2 tbsp tamarind paste
- 1 tbsp fish sauce
- 2 tbsp palm sugar
- 1 lime, juiced
- 1 tbsp crushed dried Thai chilli
- 2 tbsp minced shallots
- ¼ cup spring onions, chopped
- ¼ cup coriander, chopped
- 1 tbsp toasted rice powder

**To serve**
- White rice
- Cucumbers
- Spring onions

## Method

1. **Marinade:** blend the garlic, shallot, coriander (with stems), palm sugar, soy sauce, sweet
   soy sauce, fish sauce, white pepper, turmeric, coriander seeds and oil in a food processor
   until smooth and loose.
2. **Marinate:** rub the marinade all over the chicken, working it into every crevice and under
   the skin. Cover and refrigerate for at least 1 hour, or overnight for best results.
3. **Dipping sauce:** stir together the tamarind, fish sauce, palm sugar, lime juice, chilli,
   shallots, spring onions and coriander until the sugar dissolves. Stir in the toasted rice
   powder and adjust to taste.
4. **Grill:** heat the grill to 175–190°C (350–375°F). Cook the thighs skin side down first,
   4–5 minutes per side, turning as needed so the sugars in the marinade don't burn and the skin
   turns a glossy mahogany.
5. **Check doneness:** take them off at an internal temperature of 77–79°C (170–175°F). Thighs are
   safe at 74°C (165°F), but the higher temperature breaks down connective tissue so they're more
   tender.
6. **Rest and serve:** rest for 5 minutes, then slice. Serve with rice and cucumbers, topped with
   spring onions, with the nam jim jaew for dipping.

## Notes

- No coriander seeds? Use extra coriander stems instead.
- Toasted rice powder: toast uncooked sticky rice and grind it, or buy it ready-made.
- Fresh tamarind pulp, loosened with a little warm water, can replace the paste.
- The author uses a food processor rather than a traditional mortar and pestle for the marinade.
