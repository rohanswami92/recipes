## Ingredients

- 2 large onions, cut into wedges
- 2 carrots, peeled, rolling-cut (rangiri)
- 3 Yukon gold potatoes, peeled and quartered
- 1 tsp grated ginger
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- ½ apple, peeled and grated
- 1½ lb boneless, skinless chicken thighs, cut into bite-sized pieces (or rotisserie chicken —
  see Notes)
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 1½ tbsp neutral oil (vegetable, rice bran, canola, etc.)
- 4 cups chicken stock/broth
- 1 tbsp honey
- 1 tbsp soy sauce
- 1 tbsp ketchup
- 1 package (7–8 oz / 200–230 g) Japanese curry roux
- 8 servings cooked Japanese short-grain rice
- Fukujinzuke (Japanese red pickled vegetables), to serve

## Method

**Prepare the ingredients**

1. Cut the onions into thick wedges (or slice thinner if you'd rather they melt into the sauce).
2. Peel the carrots and cut into rolling wedges (rangiri) — this creates more surface area so
   they absorb flavor and cook faster.
3. Peel and quarter the potatoes, then soak in water for 15 minutes to remove excess starch.
   Yukon gold holds its shape well; russets tend to disintegrate, so add them later if using.
4. Grate the ginger and mince the garlic.
5. Peel, core, and grate the apple.
6. Trim excess fat from the chicken and cut into bite-sized pieces on a diagonal (sogigiri) for
   more surface area. Season with black pepper. (See Notes for a rotisserie chicken shortcut.)

**Cook the curry**

7. Heat the oil in a large pot over medium heat and add the onions. Sauté, stirring
   occasionally, until translucent and tender, about 5 minutes — don't stir too often or they
   won't develop color.
8. Add the garlic and ginger and mix well.
9. Add the chicken and cook, stirring frequently, until no longer pink on the outside. Reduce
   heat to medium-low if the onions start browning too much.
10. Add the stock, then the grated apple, honey, soy sauce, and ketchup.
11. Add the carrots and potatoes. The broth should just barely cover everything — extra liquid
    will release from the meat and vegetables as it cooks.
12. Bring to a boil and skim off any scum/foam from the surface. Cover and simmer on medium-low
    for 15 minutes, stirring occasionally.
13. Continue cooking, covered, until a skewer passes easily through the carrots and potatoes.

**Add the curry roux**

14. Turn off the heat. Dissolve the curry roux a couple of cubes at a time in a ladleful of the
    cooking liquid, then stir back into the pot until fully incorporated.
15. Simmer uncovered on medium-low, stirring frequently so nothing sticks and burns on the
    bottom, until the curry thickens, about 5–10 minutes. Add water if it gets too thick.

**Serve**

16. Serve over Japanese short-grain rice, garnished with fukujinzuke if you like.

## Notes

- Ken just uses rotisserie chicken instead of cooking chicken thighs for this recipe — shred or
  chop it and stir it in near the end (once the roux is added) just to heat through, skipping
  the raw-chicken browning step entirely.
- Cutting vegetables into thick, rustic pieces (rangiri for carrots, sogigiri for chicken) is a
  classic Japanese technique that increases surface area and speeds up cooking.
- Leftovers keep in the fridge up to 3 days or in the freezer up to a month — take the potatoes
  out before freezing since their texture changes. The sauce thickens a lot as it cools, so loosen
  with a splash of water before reheating gently.
